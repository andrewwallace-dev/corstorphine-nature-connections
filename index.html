READING THE MAP

Evidence, ideas & open data

This is a working discussion map. Dashed connections are suggestions for investigation, not surveyed wildlife routes, confirmed habitats or Council-approved projects.

What is mapped evidence?

Solid green and blue shapes, streets, paths and boundary geometry come from open mapping records. Their existence in a dataset does not establish ecological quality, public access, ownership or current condition. A road is mapped evidence; its effect on a particular species is an inference.

How the opportunities were drawn

Candidate axes were manually drawn between nearby mapped green spaces, streets or neighbourhoods. They are illustrative lines, not least-cost paths or exact intervention sites. Their indicative lengths follow these lines and do not measure an ecological gap. No species movement, planting quality, garden access or flowering continuity has been verified. There is no universal bee-flight threshold applied here.

Garden wildlife links identify areas for neighbour engagement. Domestic gardens are incompletely mapped; no private garden is assumed to be accessible. The map does not contain a verified hedgehog population, fence-hole inventory or a complete tree canopy layer. Blank areas mean missing information, not absence of habitat.

Policy & practical guidance

  • Edinburgh Nature Network — supports connected habitats, improved verges and community involvement. Its official action polygons have not been imported; these candidates need checking against the Council’s network.
  • Edinburgh Biodiversity Action Plan 2022–2027 — context for habitat and species projects.
  • Buglife B-Lines — flower-rich stepping stones. The official B-Lines dataset is not shown; the amber links are local suggestions.
  • Hedgehog Street: link your garden — 13 × 13 cm openings can connect consenting neighbours’ gardens; choose safe locations away from traffic and check suitability with owners.
  • Corstorphine in Bloom’s library planter — local evidence of community planting, with the candidate endpoint approximate. No full planter inventory is claimed.
  • Edinburgh Green Spaces Forum policy guide — links to wider Council policy documents.

Before making an intervention

Walk the candidate area. Record seasonal flowers, hedge gaps, shade, mowing, lighting and safe crossings. Check land ownership, utilities, permissions and maintenance. Protect pavement access and sightlines. Agree the approach with Council officers and local partners, then measure what changes.
